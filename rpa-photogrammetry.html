RPA photogrammetry · Spatial accuracy

Assessing Positional Accuracy in RPA Photogrammetry

This project investigated the positional accuracy of photogrammetric mapping produced using conventional GPS and Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) positioning.

University of Tasmania · 2026
01 / Approach

Comparing two positioning methods.

Using RPA-derived orthomosaics and digital elevation models, I compared the horizontal and vertical accuracy of both approaches against 18 independently surveyed checkpoints.

The analysis examined how different positioning methods affected the accuracy of photogrammetric mapping products, and how positional errors could be communicated through cartographic visualisation.

02 / Key results

GPS versus RTK.

Conventional GPS 0.610 m Total 3D RMSE
Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) 0.096 m Total 3D RMSE

The RTK-based dataset demonstrated an approximately 84% reduction in total 3D RMSE compared with the conventional GPS dataset.

04 / Methodology and discussion

Validating the mapping.

Two photogrammetric datasets were assessed: one using conventional GPS positioning and another using RTK positioning.

Positional errors were evaluated using 18 surveyed checkpoints distributed across the study area. Differences between mapped and surveyed positions were used to assess horizontal and vertical accuracy.

To visualise horizontal error, displacement vectors were exaggerated by a factor of 50, allowing otherwise small positional differences to be clearly displayed at the map scale.

Vertical errors were represented using graduated circles, with separate colours for GPS and RTK. This allowed differences in error magnitude and distribution to be compared spatially.

What the results showed

The results demonstrate the substantial improvement in positional accuracy achievable through RTK positioning.

The project also highlights the importance of independent checkpoint validation and clear cartographic communication of spatial uncertainty in RPA-derived mapping products.
